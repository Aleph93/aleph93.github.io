Moonscars

Featured
Years
2018 – 2024

Black Mermaid
Genre
2D Action Platformer
Roles

Platforms

Moonscars is a commercially released 2D action-platformer inspired by the Soulslike and Metroidvania genres.

As the sole programmer, I was responsible for all technical aspects of the project from early prototypes through release. Over 4 years of development and 2 years of post-launch support, I designed, implemented, and maintained the systems required to ship a complete commercial game.

These included gameplay systems, UI, save/load functionality, custom editor tooling, localization support, level streaming, Nintendo Switch porting, and overall software architecture. I also designed and implemented a custom AI framework that powered all enemy behaviors in the game and was later extended to support dialogue trees and scripted event systems.

Beyond development, I played a key role in project planning, budgeting, milestone preparation, effort estimation, publisher communication, and production coordination. I was responsible for technical planning and decision-making throughout development, while major project direction and production priorities were determined jointly with my co-founder. I also acted as the primary technical contact for localization and external development partners supporting the game's multi-platform release.

Meetaverse

Years
2025 – 2026
Client
Althimis
Role
Unity Developer

Meetaverse is a platform for creating and hosting interactive virtual events, training activities and collaborative experiences within customizable 3D environments.

I was responsible for the development of the environment authoring tool used to create, configure, and manage these environments without developer involvement. The tool allowed users to compose environments, configure content, create reusable templates, and assign modular runtime behaviours to objects through a component-based workflow.

A major focus of my work was the data pipeline responsible for serializing environment data, downloading content and configuration from remote services, and dynamically reconstructing complete environments at runtime. This included support for hierarchical scene structures, object references, modular configurable components, and runtime asset loading. I also coordinated closely with engineering teams that work on client, backend and communication technologies.

DreamWorks Color

Year
2016
Client
DreamWorks
Genre
Augmented Reality mobile application
Tech
Unity3D (C#), Vuforia
Role
Unity Developer
Platforms
iOS, Android

DreamWorks Color was an Augmented Reality mobile application that allowed children to bring their colored DreamWorks characters to life. By scanning specially designed coloring pages, users could view animated 3D characters in AR with their own coloring applied to the models.

Working as part of a two-programmer team, I was responsible for the image-processing pipeline that transformed scanned coloring pages into textures that could be applied to 3D characters at runtime. This involved extracting and correcting images captured by the device camera, compensating for perspective distortion, and generating textures suitable for use on animated 3D models.

The project provided valuable experience with AR development, image processing, texture manipulation and mobile optimization using Unity and Vuforia.

VR Development Framework & VR Experiences

Year
2016
Tech
Unity3D, HTC Vive (SteamVR)
Role
Unity Developer

Developed a reusable VR framework for HTC Vive projects that was adopted across multiple applications within the company.

The framework addressed two major challenges of early VR development: limited access to headset hardware and the complexity of the SteamVR SDK. Its core feature was a recording and playback system that allowed developers to capture headset and controller interactions once, then replay them directly inside the Unity Editor for testing and debugging without requiring VR hardware. The framework also provided a simplified abstraction layer over SteamVR, reducing implementation complexity and standardizing common interaction patterns.

The framework was subsequently used as the foundation for multiple VR experiences, including EventTech: BeCreative and Wonder Woman VR, where I served as the sole programmer responsible for gameplay systems and feature implementation.

Chrono Distortion

Year
2010
Context
School project
Genre
3D first-person puzzle
Tech
OpenGL (C++), FMOD
Role
Everything

Chrono Distortion is a 3D first-person action game tightly mixed with puzzle elements. To complete a level, the player has to get through obstacles (doors) that can be opened using a series of buttons triggered by the player or by special boxes. The protagonist is able to manipulate time in a number of ways, including a complete stop, rewinding, and projecting himself in time.

Being able to re-live the same moment again and again, each time from a different perspective, gives the player the ability to see themselves in real time and act in symbiosis with their past projections to solve the puzzles and open access to the next level.
